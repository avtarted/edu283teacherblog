Culture of Power

Being a teacher comes with enormous responsibility. My actions as a teacher will shape student outcomes. And it is important that all students succeed in my classroom, not just students from upper-middle-class families that have power in society.

Enter Delpit’s (1988[1]) Culture of Power. The rules and codes of how to operate in both society and the classroom are set by those in power. So students from non-middle class families who lack this knowledge may have worse outcomes and thus hierarchies self-propagate through schooling. As a teacher, I need to counter this and start by acknowledging my own membership to power.

I personally come from a middle class family. I took advanced track courses, had private piano and badminton instructors, and attended a prestigious college. Undoubtedly I must have internalized many rules and attitudes of power. So I need to question any negative reactions I have to students not following these rules and learn more about my students’ cultures.

Universal Design for Learning

CAST created the Universal Design for Learning (UDL) framework to support teachers and students. Their framework is scientifically informed and recognizes diversity in learners (CAST)[1]. They also created the UDL Guidelines that have three principles to design multiple means for: Engagement, Representation, and Action & Expression (CAST)[2].

Math is a somewhat "universal" discipline, so as a Math teacher, I'll have plentiful options of integrating these UDL Guidelines in my instruction.

Engagement: I'll survey my students and learn what their interests are, so that I can create relevant assignments. I'll give open ended projects such as the Differential Calculus group project where groups get to choose their own project.

Representation: Math lends itself especially well to a wide variety of representations that I will utilize such as visualizations through drawings, diagrams, graphs in addition to typical symbolic notation.

Action & Expression: I'll employ problem-solving scaffolds such as sequential graphic organizers where I'll break problems down into individual sub-tasks. I'll focus not just at the problem at hand but the process as well so that students are able to plan out their own approaches to solving problems.

Restorative Practice

In traditional classrooms and, more broadly in our legal system, it's all about enforcing consequences conditioned on the particular infraction an individual caused. Sure, these punitive practices may deter behavior, but they may not lead to closure. Moreover, punishments that involve extended time outside the classroom are especially problematic, as they may be differentially enforced and result in lower graduation rates and future academic and career prospects. Restorative Practices, on the other hand, seek to restore and repair harm and learning from our mistakes. They involve teacher or other facilitator mediated conversations with impacted students and potentially their families and members of the community where everyone takes turns, explains their feelings, and come together to seek resolution.

The most important part of this practice, to me, is self-reflection and empathy. If you do something wrong and pay a fine, are things really resolved? Is this just another "cost of doing business" matter? The goal should be to reflect on what you did wrong, realize your action's impact on others, and take accountability for it. While I may not host these restorative practice circles, I will hear from everyone involved, try to understand their thought processes, motivations, and emotions and have students self-reflect and articulate what they could have done differently.
